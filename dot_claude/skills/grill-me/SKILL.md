# Grill Me

Run a `/grilling` session, extended with the two directions below.
The base protocol stands — the decisions remain the user's; where an extension qualifies the base, the extension prevails.

## 1. A decision is asked only from a formed position

The base already forbids asking for facts you could look up; hold decisions to the same bar.
Investigate the artifacts first, and bring every question with a properly chosen suggestion and the reasoning behind it — the recommendation of a fully intelligent, autonomous party, not a menu of unweighed options.
Ask only when the answer changes what gets built and defensible answers genuinely diverge; assert a convention-default with a veto window instead of asking it.
An options menu where every option carries your own recommendation is delegated sign-off, not a question.

## 2. Grilling aims at unexplored problem space, not choice inventory

The session succeeds by what it reveals — walking a decision checklist where nothing new is seen has failed.
Hunt where unseen problems live: silent shared "of course" assumptions; contradictions between plan, code, data, and earlier decisions; unmeasurable success claims (measured how, by whom, from what data?); lifecycle and boundary blind spots (failure vs. silence, staleness, concurrency, supersession, who-watches-the-watcher); future-consumer gaps (what a later stage or the stated end-goal needs that today's shape forecloses); conclusions anchored on sunk cost, continuity, or popularity rather than merit.
Present each finding as a challenge, one at a time — the assumption, the evidence, the consequence if wrong, and your position offered for veto — and treat pushback as new grill material, not resistance.

Close with a ledger: each revealed assumption verified, consciously accepted as residual risk, or converted into a decision or deferral.
